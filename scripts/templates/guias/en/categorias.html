Stop game categories: easy, hard and themed ideas

Categories make or break a game of Stop (also known as Categories or Tutti Frutti). Play the same ones every time and it gets predictable; switch them up and every round feels new. Here are more than 90 ideas sorted by difficulty and theme, ready for a paper sheet or an online room.

How to choose categories for a game

A good round mixes categories everyone can fill in with a few that make people think. A rule of thumb that works: six to eight categories per round, half of them easy and the rest medium or hard. If you're playing with kids or first-timers, keep them all easy; if your group is experienced, add two or three hard ones so the same person doesn't win every time.

It also helps to think about who's playing. A group of sports fans will love Athlete; a family game with grandparents and grandkids works better with something like Thing in the kitchen. And if someone in the group lives in another country, avoid categories that are too local or accept answers from their region.

How to use these categories in tutti.frutti

When you create a private room you can pick categories one by one, load a quick pack (Classic, Argentine culture or Random, which draws new categories every round) or type your own categories with any name you like. That means you can copy any idea from this page and set up a themed game in seconds.

Random rooms, on the other hand, always use the eight classic categories, because two strangers have no way to agree on them before starting.

If an answer is questionable, the game has a challenge system and an AI judge that decides whether the word belongs to the category. You can read the details in the Stop game rules.
